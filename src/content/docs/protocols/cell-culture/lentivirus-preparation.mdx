## Plating (day 1)

HEK293T cells are generally plated at 2×10<sup>5</sup>/ml.  
The exception are 10cm dishes where we reduce the culture volume from the usual 15ml to 10ml to reduce the amount of transfection reagent, bringing the concentration to 3×10<sup>5</sup>/ml.

| | 96-well (0.35 cm²) | 48-well (0.9 cm²) | 24-well (1.9 cm²) | 12-well (3.8 cm²) | 6-well (9.6 cm²) | 10 cm dish (59 cm²) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume | 0.1 ml | 0.3 ml | 0.6 ml | 1.2 ml | 2.5 ml | 10 ml |
| Cell number | 0.2×10<sup>5</sup> | 0.6×10<sup>5</sup> | 1.2×10<sup>5</sup> | 2.4×10<sup>5</sup> | 5×10<sup>5</sup> | 3×10<sup>6</sup> |
| Cell suspension | 2×10<sup>5</sup>/ml | 2×10<sup>5</sup>/ml | 2×10<sup>5</sup>/ml | 2×10<sup>5</sup>/ml | 2×10<sup>5</sup>/ml | 3×10<sup>5</sup>/ml |

:::tip[Seeding density depends on time to transfection]
Aim for a confluency around 80 % at transfection. Seeding density may have to be adjusted depending on time from plating to transfection. For example, for 96- to 6-well plates:
- **~24 h**: plate at ~2×10<sup>5</sup>/ml.
- **~12 h**: plate at the higher density, ~3×10<sup>5</sup>/ml.
:::

## Transfection (day 1 evening or day 2 morning)
Confluency should be around 80 % at transfection. Use the EVOS M3000 to objectively measure confluency and document this in your lab notebook.
### Procedure
We use TransIT-LT1 as a transfection reagent but adapt the protocol
in that we usually prepare separate DNA and TransIT dilutions which are then mixed (as in the Lipofectamine 2000 protocol):
1. Dilute the DNA in Optimem (half of the final volume, e.g. 125 µl for 6-well).
2. Dilute TransIT in Optimem (half of the final volume, e.g. 125 µl for 6-well) and incubate for 5 min (max. 30 min).
3. Combine both dilutions, mix gently and incubate at room temperature for 15–30 min.
4. Apply the TransIT:DNA complexes to the cells drop-wise, to different areas of the well, and distribute by rocking the plate gently.

:::tip[Handling TransIT-LT1]
- Warm TransIT-LT1 and Opti-MEM to room temperature before use, to maximize complex formation.
- Vortex TransIT-LT1 gently before each use.
- Do not let the reagent touch the wall of the plastic tube when adding it to the diluted DNA.
:::

### Amounts
The DNA to TransIT-LT1 ratio is 1 µg : 3 µl.
The standard pMD2.G : psPAX2 : transfer vector ratio is 1:3:4.
The 10 cm dish amounts here deviate from the TransIT-LT1 protocol, because of the reduced culture volume we
use at this step (10 ml instead of 15 ml).
The reasoning behind these choices, and when to use something else, is in [Protocol notes and open questions](#protocol-notes-and-open-questions).

| | 96-well (0.35 cm²) | 48-well (0.9 cm²) | 24-well (1.9 cm²) | 12-well (3.8 cm²) | 6-well (9.6 cm²) | 10 cm dish (59 cm²) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pMD2.G (EX01) | 12 ng | 36 ng | 70 ng | 140 ng | 300 ng | 1.25 µg |
| psPAX2 (EX02) | 36 ng | 108 ng | 210 ng | 420 ng | 900 ng | 3.75 µg |
| transfer plasmid | 48 ng | 144 ng | 280 ng | 560 ng | 1200 ng | 5 µg |

### Mastermixes

Mastermixes for steps 1 and 2 are usually set up as follows (6-well example):

| DNA mastermix | 1-fold | 6-fold |
|---|---|---|
| psPAX2 | 900 ng | 5400 ng |
| pMD2.G | 300 ng | 1800 ng |
| transfer plasmid (pLKO) | 1200 ng | added to each condition individually |
| Optimem | 125 µl | 750 µl |

The DNA:Optimem mastermix for packaging and envelope plasmids is distributed to individual tubes (6-well: 125 µl per condition), and the indicated amount of transfer vector is added.

| TransIT mastermix | 1-fold | 6-fold |
|---|---|---|
| TransIT-LT1 | 7.2 µl | 43.2 µl |
| Optimem | 125 µl | 750 µl |

5. After ~5 min incubation (max. 30 min) of the TransIT:Optimem mastermix, add the indicated amount (6-well: 125 µl) to each DNA:Optimem mix and incubate at room temperature for 15–30 min.
6. Add the TransIT:DNA:Optimem mix to the cells, distributing it as evenly as possible.

## Medium change (day 2)

12–18 h after transfection, change the medium to virus-production medium (DMEM, 20 % FBS, 1 % P/S; volumes below).

| | 96-well | 48-well | 24-well | 12-well | 6-well | 10 cm dish |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume per supernatant change | 125 µl | 300 µl | 750 µl | 1.5 ml | 3 ml | 15 ml |
| Final / total supernatant volume | 250 µl | 600 µl | 1.5 ml | 3 ml | 6 ml | 30 ml |

## Collect supernatant 1 (day 3)

1. Transfer the supernatant (SN1) to a collection tube (15 ml or 50 ml) and store at 4 °C.
2. Replace with fresh virus-production medium.

:::note
SN1 usually has a higher titer than SN2.
:::


## Collect supernatant 2 (day 4)

1. Transfer the supernatant (SN2) to the collection tube, pooling it with SN1.

## Clearing the supernatant

At this point the lentivirus supernatant can be used for a **fresh infection**, or **concentrated** for storage and later use. Fresh infection is the most efficient and fastest way to transduce cells. Either way, the HEK293T cells are first removed from the supernatant:

- **Clearing by centrifugation:** spin the supernatant at 800 × g for 10 min (MD Anderson) or up to 3000 × g for 15 min (Retro-Concentin) at room temperature.
- **Clearing by filtration:** pass the supernatant through a yellow 0.45 µm Millipore filter.

Carefully transfer the cleared supernatant into a new tube.

## Virus concentration (optional, day 4–5)

Follows the homemade lentivirus concentrator protocol of the MD Anderson Functional Genomics Core.
The 4× stock is made in-house: see [4× Lentivirus Concentrator Solution](/protocols/buffers-and-recipes/lentivirus-concentrator/).

1. Add **1 volume of 4× concentrator to 3 volumes of cleared supernatant** (final: 10 % PEG-8000, 0.3 M NaCl).
2. Mix well by shaking for 60 s, then incubate at 4 °C for at least 4 h, better overnight.
3. Spin down at **1600 × g for 60 min at 4 °C**.
4. Carefully remove the supernatant without disturbing the pellet.
5. Thoroughly resuspend the viral pellet in PBS or the desired medium (without serum and antibiotics) at 1/10–1/20 of the original volume, by gently pipetting up and down.
6. Aliquot and store at −80 °C until use.

:::note
The MD Anderson protocol calls for constant rocking at ~60 rpm during the 4 °C incubation.
In our hands this does not appear to be required but we haven't tested it side-by-side.
:::

## Protocol notes and open questions
Numerous protocols are available for lentivirus production.
Most follow the same basic workflow but vary in their choice of transfection reagent and in the ratios of the plasmids used.  
Our protocol mainly follows the RNAi Consortium (TRC) / Broad Institute protocol for lentivirus production.
The Broad protocol is very detailed and, judging by its version notes, has been continuously optimized. It is available at the [Broad GPP portal](https://portals.broadinstitute.org/gpp/public/resources/protocols) (*TRC shRNA sgRNA ORF Low Throughput Viral Production, 2015-06*).  

### Comparison of published protocols
The ratio of envelope to packaging to transfer plasmid ranges widely in published protocols, from 1 : 1.8 : 2.8 to 1 : 10 : 10.  
To our knowledge, there is no experimentally established optimum.  

| | Broad (6-well) | Broad (10 cm) | Trono lab (15 cm) | Melnick lab (10 cm) | Addgene (6 cm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| envelope (pMD2.G) | 50 ng | 0.9 µg | 7.9 µg | 1 µg | 250 ng |
| packaging (psPAX2) | 500 ng | 9 µg | 14.6 µg | 3 µg | 750 ng |
| transfer (pLKO) | 500 ng | 9 µg | 22.5 µg | 4 µg | 1000 ng |
| ratio | 1 : 10 : 10 | 1 : 10 : 10 | 1 : 1.8 : 2.8 | 1 : 3 : 4 | 1 : 3 : 4 |
| Transfection reagent | 3 µl (TransIT) | 54 µl (TransIT) | CaPO<sub>4</sub> | 24 µl (FuGENE) | 6 µl (FuGENE) |
| DNA : reagent | 1 µg : 3 µl | 1 µg : 2.7 µl | n/a | 1 µg : 3 µl | 1 µg : 3 µl |

The Trono lab protocol is written for 15 cm dishes and gives its amounts as one mix for five plates
(39.5 / 73 / 112.5 µg); the column above is that mix divided by five. Their packaging plasmid is pCMVR8.74
rather than psPAX2.

### What we use and why
**Transfection reagent**  
Commonly used transfection reagents include Lipofectamine 2000, FuGENE and TransIT-LT1.
We have used all of them and although we have not done a direct head-to-head comparison, they seem to be interchangeable.
We use TransIT-LT1 mainly for economic reasons.  

**Ratios**  
- We use a 1 : 3 : 4 ratio for envelope (pMD2.G) to packaging (psPAX2) to transfer plasmid. This follows the Melnick lab protocol and the Addgene
protocol for pLKO.1 (#10878).
- For larger transfer plasmids (e.g. Cas9), a ratio of 1 : 3 : 6 may be used. However, we have not systematically tested these ratios.  
- We use TransIT-LT1 at 1 µg DNA : 3 µl reagent. This is the manufacturer's recommended starting ratio.


**When not to use TransIT-LT1**  
TransIT-LT1 is expensive and the cost scales with the dish. At 10 cm and above, reserve it for cargo that is
large or hard to package — Cas constructs and CRISPR gRNA libraries — where
transfection efficiency has to be maximized and a failed prep costs more than the reagent. For routine
large-scale preps, use PEI or calcium-phosphate transfection instead: both cost a fraction as much and work
well with ordinary inserts.
Large transfer vectors package less efficiently in general, which is also why the 1 : 3 : 6 ratio exists as an
option for them (below).

**10 cm dish protocols**  
The amounts of DNA and transfection reagent for a 10 cm dish are not scaled up proportionally from the 6-well
protocol, but reduced relative to the TransIT-LT1 manufacturer's protocol. The reduction follows the Melnick
lab protocol and is probably feasible because we use a smaller culture volume at this step (10 ml rather than
15 ml).

- Low intensity I (1:3:4) is our standard and is the column in the main transfection table: 10 µg total DNA and 30 µl TransIT-LT1.
- For large transfer vectors, intensifying the transfection and/or changing the ratios can help.
- High intensity (45 µl TransIT) corresponds to the TransIT manufacturer's protocol.
- Use a 1:3:6 ratio for large transfer vectors (e.g. Cas9 constructs).

| | low intensity I (1:3:4) | low intensity II (1:3:6) | moderate intensity (1:3:4) | high intensity (1:3:4) | high intensity (1:3:6) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pMD2.G (EX01) | 1.25 µg | 1 µg | 1.5 µg | 1.9 µg | 1.5 µg |
| psPAX2 (EX02) | 3.75 µg | 3 µg | 4.5 µg | 5.7 µg | 4.5 µg |
| transfer plasmid | 5 µg | 6 µg | 6 µg | 7.6 µg | 9 µg |
| **total DNA** | 10 µg | 10 µg | 12 µg | ~15 µg | 15 µg |
| TransIT-LT1 | 30 µl | 30 µl | 36 µl | 45 µl | 45 µl |
| Optimem (total) | 1.5 ml | 1.5 ml | 1.5 ml | 1.5 ml | 1.5 ml |



### Open questions

- **Virus-production medium:** the Broad protocol suggests adding BSA instead of FBS to enhance virus production.
This would be highly economical but we have not tested this ourselves.
- **Ratio of envelope to packaging to transfer plasmid**: published protocols vary widely and we have not yet tested this systematically.
- **Constant rocking** during the concentrator incubation does not appear to be necessary (see
  [Virus concentration](#virus-concentration-optional-day-45)), but this has not been tested side by side.
